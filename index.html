About

I am a graduate student at the University of Pittsburgh, where I am pursuing an M.S. in Mathematics and am currently a Ph.D. student in Economics. My M.S. thesis is advised by Juan J. Manfredi and concerns partial differential equations and stochastic tug-of-war games.

I am also currently working with Irene Fonseca at Carnegie Mellon University on problems in the calculus of variations involving relaxation, lower semicontinuity, and variable-coefficient PDE constraints.

Current Research

Calculus of Variations and Variable-Coefficient PDE Constraints

Carnegie Mellon University  •  Irene Fonseca  •  2026–present

I study relaxation, lower semicontinuity, and A-quasiconvexity for variational problems subject to variable-coefficient differential constraints.

My current work develops a moving-kernel approach to variable-coefficient A-free oscillations, with the goal of obtaining necessity results without pseudodifferential machinery and extending global relaxation methods to the variable-coefficient setting.

Nonlinear PDE and Stochastic Tug-of-War Games

M.S. Thesis Research  •  University of Pittsburgh  •  Juan J. Manfredi

I study nonlinear elliptic equations through stochastic tug-of-war games and asymptotic mean value characterizations, with particular emphasis on the regularized p-Laplacian.

This work includes projected tug-of-war games and asymptotic mean value formulas associated with nonlinear elliptic equations, interpreted in the viscosity-solution framework.

Related Research

My broader research has included optimal control, random-set geometry, partial identification, differential privacy, and simulation-based statistical inference. This work has led to collaborations with Arie Beresteanu and Jordan Awan .
